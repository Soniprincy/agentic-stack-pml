Chemical compound
[Caprolactam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caprolactam "Caprolactam") molecule used to synthesize Nylon 6 by [ring opening polymerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_opening_polymerization "Ring opening polymerization")
**Nylon 6** or **polycaprolactam** is a [polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer "Polymer"), in particular [semicrystalline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semicrystalline "Semicrystalline") [polyamide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyamide "Polyamide"). Unlike most other [nylons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon "Nylon"), nylon 6 is not a [condensation polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensation_polymer "Condensation polymer"), but instead is formed by [ring-opening polymerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring-opening_polymerization "Ring-opening polymerization"); this makes it a special case in the comparison between condensation and [addition polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addition_polymer "Addition polymer"). Its competition with [nylon 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon_66 "Nylon 66") and the example it set have also shaped the economics of the [synthetic fibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fibre "Synthetic fibre") industry. It is sold under numerous trade names including Perlon (Germany), Dederon (former East Germany), Nylatron, Capron, Ultramid, Akulon, Kapron (former Soviet Union and satellite states), Rugopa (Turkey) and Durethan.
## History
Polycaprolactam was developed by [Paul Schlack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Schlack "Paul Schlack") at [IG Farben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Farben "IG Farben") in late 1930s (first synthesized in 1938) to reproduce the properties of [Nylon 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon_66 "Nylon 66") without violating the [patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent "Patent") on its production. (Around the same time, Kohei Hoshino at [Toray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toray "Toray") also succeeded in synthesizing nylon 6.) It was marketed as Perlon, and industrial production with a capacity of 3,500 tons per year was established in Nazi Germany in 1943, using phenol as a feedstock. At first, the polymer was used to produce coarse fiber for artificial bristle, then the fiber quality was improved, and Germans started making parachutes, cord for aircraft tires and towing cables for gliders.
The Soviet Union began its development of an analog in the 1940s, while negotiating with Germany on building an IG Farben plant in Ukraine,[] basic scientific work was ongoing in 1942. The production only started in 1948 in [Klin, Moscow Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klin,_Klinsky_District,_Moscow_Oblast "Klin, Klinsky District, Moscow Oblast"), after USSR obtained the 2000 volumes of IG Farben, and 10,000 volumes of [AEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEG_\(German_company\) "AEG \(German company\)") technical documentation, as a result of victory in World War II.
## Synthesis
Nylon 6 can be modified using comonomers or stabilizers during polymerization to introduce new chain end or functional groups, which changes the reactivity and chemical properties. It is often done to change its dyeability or flame retardance. Nylon 6 is synthesized by [ring-opening polymerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring-opening_polymerization "Ring-opening polymerization") of [caprolactam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caprolactam "Caprolactam"). Caprolactam has 6 carbons, hence _Nylon 6_. When caprolactam is heated at about 533[K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin "Kelvin") in an inert [atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere "Atmosphere") of [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen "Nitrogen") for about 4–5 hours, the ring breaks and undergoes [polymerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerization "Polymerization"). Then the molten mass is passed through [spinnerets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinneret_\(polymers\) "Spinneret \(polymers\)") to form fibres of nylon 6.
Polymerization of caprolactam to Nylon 6.
During [polymerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerization "Polymerization"), the amide bond within each caprolactam [molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule "Molecule") is broken, with the active groups on each side re-forming two new bonds as the [monomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monomer "Monomer") becomes part of the polymer backbone. Unlike nylon 6,6, in which the direction of the [amide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amide "Amide") bond reverses at each bond, all nylon 6 amide bonds lie in the same direction (see figure: note the N to C orientation of each amide bond).

## Properties
Nylon 6 fibres are tough, possessing high tensile strength, elasticity and lustre. They are wrinkleproof and highly resistant to abrasion and chemicals such as acids and alkalis. Nylon 6 is hygroscopic, absorbing up to 2.4% of its weight in water. This has a significant effect on its properties, greatly decreasing the modulus and yield stress but increasing the ductility. The glass transition temperature of Nylon 6 is 47°C.
As a synthetic fibre, Nylon 6 is generally white but can be dyed in a solution bath prior to production for different color results. Its [tenacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenacity_\(textile_strength\) "Tenacity \(textile strength\)") is 6–8.5[gf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram-force "Gram-force")/[D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_textile_measurement#Denier "Units of textile measurement") with a density of 1.14g/cm3. Its melting point is at 215°C and can protect heat up to 150°C on average.
## Biodegradation
_Flavobacterium_ sp. [85] and _Pseudomonas_ sp. (NK87) degrade [oligomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligomer "Oligomer") of Nylon 6, but not polymers. Certain [white rot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_rot "White rot") fungal strains can also degrade Nylon 6 through oxidation. Compared to aliphatic polyesters, Nylon 6 has been said to have poor [biodegradability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodegradability "Biodegradability"). Strong interchain interactions from hydrogen bonds between molecular nylon chains is said to be the cause by some sources.
However, in 2023 a team of [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University "Northwestern University") chemists led by [Linda Broadbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Broadbelt "Linda Broadbelt") and [Tobin J. Marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobin_J._Marks "Tobin J. Marks") developed rare earth [metallocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallocene "Metallocene") catalysts that rapidly break Nylon 6 down back to caprolactam at 220°C, which is considered mild conditions.
## Production in Europe
At present, polyamide 6 is a significant construction material used in many industries, for instance in the automotive industry, aircraft industry, electronic and electrotechnical industry, clothing industry and medicine. Annual demand for polyamides in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") amounts to a million [tonnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonne "Tonne"). They are produced by all leading chemical companies.
The largest producers of polyamide 6 in Europe:
  1. [Fibrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibrant?action=edit&redlink=1 "Fibrant \(page does not exist\)"), 260,000 tonnes per year
  2. [BASF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASF "BASF"), 240,000 tonnes per year
  3. [Lanxess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanxess "Lanxess"), 170,000 tonnes per year
  4. [Radici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radici_Group "Radici Group"), 125,000 tonnes per year
  5. [DOMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DOMO_Group "DOMO Group"), 100,000 tonnes per year
  6. [Grupa Azoty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupa_Azoty_S.A. "Grupa Azoty S.A."), 100,000 tonnes per year
