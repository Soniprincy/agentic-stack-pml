**6061 aluminium alloy** ([Unified Numbering System (UNS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_numbering_system "Unified numbering system") designation A96061) is a [precipitation-hardened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_hardening "Precipitation hardening") [aluminium alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium_alloy "Aluminium alloy"), containing [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium "Magnesium") and [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon "Silicon") as its major [alloying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy "Alloy") elements. Originally called "Alloy 61S", it was developed in 1935. It has good mechanical properties, exhibits good [weldability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weldability "Weldability"), and is very commonly [extruded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrusion "Extrusion") (second in popularity only to [6063](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6063_aluminium_alloy "6063 aluminium alloy")). It is one of the most common alloys of [aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium "Aluminium") for general-purpose use.
It is commonly available in pre-tempered grades such as 6061-O (annealed), tempered grades such as 6061-T6 (solutionized and artificially aged) and 6061-T651 (solutionized, stress-relieved stretched and artificially aged).

## Properties
The mechanical properties of 6061 greatly depend on the [temper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempering_\(metallurgy\) "Tempering \(metallurgy\)"), or heat treatment, of the material. [Young's Modulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young's_Modulus "Young's Modulus") is 69GPa (10,000ksi) regardless of temper.
### 6061-O
[Annealed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annealing_\(metallurgy\) "Annealing \(metallurgy\)") 6061 (6061-O temper) has maximum [ultimate tensile strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_tensile_strength "Ultimate tensile strength") no more than 150MPa (22ksi), and maximum [yield strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yield_strength "Yield strength") no more than 83MPa (12ksi) for sheets and plates or 110MPa (16ksi) for bars, rods and wires. The material has elongation (stretch before ultimate failure) of 10–18%. To obtain the annealed condition, the alloy is typically heat soaked at 415°C for 2–3 hours.
### 6061-T4
T4 temper 6061 has an [ultimate tensile strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_tensile_strength "Ultimate tensile strength") of at least 210MPa (30ksi) for sheets and plates or 180MPa (26ksi) for bars, rods and wires, and yield strength of at least 110MPa (16ksi). It has elongation of 10-16%.
### 6061-T6
6061-T6 aluminium standard heat treating process
T6 temper 6061 has been treated to provide the maximum precipitation hardening (and therefore maximum yield strength) for a 6061 aluminium alloy. It has an ultimate tensile strength of at least 290MPa (42ksi) and yield strength of at least 240MPa (35ksi). More typical values are 310MPa (45ksi) and 270MPa (39ksi), respectively. This can exceed the yield strength of certain types of [stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainless_steel "Stainless steel"). In thicknesses of 6.35mm (0.250in) or less, it has elongation of 8% or more; in thicker sections, it has elongation of 10%. T651 temper has similar mechanical properties. The typical value for thermal conductivity for 6061-T6 at 25°C (77°F) is around 152 W/m K. The [fatigue limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatigue_limit "Fatigue limit") under cyclic load is 97MPa (14ksi) for 500,000,000 completely reversed cycles using a standard RR Moore test machine and specimen. Note that aluminium does not exhibit a well defined "knee" on its [S-N curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatigue_\(material\)#Stress-life_\(S-N\)_method "Fatigue \(material\)"), so there is some debate as to how many cycles equates to "infinite life". Also note the actual value of fatigue limit for an application can be dramatically affected by the conventional de-rating factors of loading, gradient, and surface finish.
## Microstructure
Different aluminium heat treatments control the size and dispersion of precipitates in the material. Grain boundary sizes also change, but do not have as important of an impact on strength as the precipitates. Grain sizes can change orders of magnitude based upon stress and can have grains as small as a few hundred nanometres, but are typically a few micrometres to hundreds of micrometres in diameter. Iron, manganese, and chromium secondary phases (FeSiAl, (Fe, Mn, Cr)SiAl) often form as inclusions in the material.
Grain boundaries in extruded plate 6061 aluminium alloy
Grain sizes in aluminium alloys are heavily dependent upon the processing techniques and heat treatment. Different cross-sections of material which has been stressed can cause order of magnitude differences in grain size. Some specially processed aluminium alloys have grain diameters which are hundreds of nanometres, but most range from a few micrometres to hundreds of micrometres.

6061 is commonly used for the following: 
  * construction of [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft "Aircraft") structures, such as [wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing "Wing") and [fuselages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuselage "Fuselage"), more commonly in [homebuilt aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homebuilt_aircraft "Homebuilt aircraft") than commercial or military aircraft. [2024 alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_aluminium_alloy "2024 aluminium alloy") is somewhat stronger, but 6061 is more easily worked and remains resistant to corrosion even when the surface is abraded. This is not the case for 2024, which is usually used with a thin [Alclad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alclad "Alclad") coating for corrosion resistance. 
  * [yacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yacht "Yacht") construction, including small utility [boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat "Boat"). 
  * automotive parts, such as the chassis of the [Audi A8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_A8 "Audi A8") and the [Plymouth Prowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_Prowler "Plymouth Prowler"). 
  * flashlights
  * [Scuba tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_cylinder "Diving cylinder") and other high pressure gas storage cylinders (post 1995)


6061-T6 is used for:
  * [bicycle frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_frame "Bicycle frame") and components 
  * middle to high-end [recurve risers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurve_bow "Recurve bow")
  * many [fly fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly_fishing "Fly fishing") reels.
  * the [Pioneer plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_plaque "Pioneer plaque")
  * the secondary chambers and baffle systems in firearm sound [suppressors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppressor "Suppressor") (primarily pistol suppressors for reduced weight and improved mechanical functionality), while the primary expansion chambers usually require 17-4PH or 303 [stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainless_steel "Stainless steel") or [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium "Titanium"). 
  * the upper and lower receivers of many non mil-spec [AR-15 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AR-15_rifle "AR-15 rifle") variants.
  * many aluminium docks and gangways, welded into place.
  * material used in some [ultra-high vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-high_vacuum "Ultra-high vacuum") (UHV) chambers 
  * many parts for remote controlled [model aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_aircraft "Model aircraft"), notably [helicopter rotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_rotor "Helicopter rotor") components.
  * large [amateur radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio "Amateur radio") antennas.
  * fire department rescue ladders


### Welding
6061 is highly weldable, for example using [tungsten inert gas welding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten_inert_gas_welding "Tungsten inert gas welding") (TIG) or [metal inert gas welding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_inert_gas_welding "Metal inert gas welding") (MIG). Typically, after welding, the properties near the weld are those of 6061-T4, a loss of strength of around 40%. The material can be re-heat-treated to restore near -T6 temper for the whole piece. After welding, the material can naturally age and restore some of its strength as well. Most strength is recovered in the first few days to a few weeks. Nevertheless, the Aluminum Design Manual (Aluminum Association) recommends the design strength of the material adjacent to the weld to be taken as 165 MPa/24000 PSI without proper heat treatment after the welding. Typical filler material is 4043 or 5356.
### Extrusions
6061 is an alloy used in the production of [extrusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrusion "Extrusion")—long constant–cross-section structural shapes produced by pushing metal through a shaped [die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_\(manufacturing\) "Die \(manufacturing\)").
**Cold and Hot Stamping**
6061 sheet in the T4 condition can be formed with limited ductility in the cold state. For deep draw and complex shapes, and for the avoidance of spring-back, an aluminium hot stamping process ([Hot Form Quench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Form_Quench?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hot Form Quench \(page does not exist\)")) can be used, which forms a blank at an elevated temperature (~ 550 C) in a cooled die, leaving a part in W-temper condition before artificial aging to the T6 full strength state.
### Forgings
6061 is an alloy that is suitable for hot [forging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forging "Forging"). The billet is heated through an [induction furnace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induction_furnace "Induction furnace") and forged using a closed die process. This particular alloy is suitable for open die forgings. Automotive parts, ATV parts, and industrial parts are just some of the uses as a forging. Aluminium 6061 can be forged into flat or round bars, rings, blocks, discs and blanks, hollows, and spindles. 6061 can be forged into special and custom shapes.
### Castings
6061 is not an alloy that is traditionally cast due to its low silicon content affecting the fluidity in casting. It can be suitably cast using a specialized [centrifugal casting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrifugal_casting_\(industrial\) "Centrifugal casting \(industrial\)") method. Centrifugally cast 6061 is ideal for larger rings and sleeve applications that exceed the limitations of most wrought offerings.
