**Polycarbonates** (**PC**) are a group of [thermoplastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoplastic "Thermoplastic") polymers containing [carbonate groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonate_ester "Carbonate ester") in their chemical structures. Polycarbonates used in engineering are strong, [tough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toughness "Toughness") materials, and some grades are optically transparent. They are easily worked, [molded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injection_molding "Injection molding"), and [thermoformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoforming "Thermoforming"). Because of these properties, polycarbonates find many applications. Polycarbonates do not have a unique [resin identification code (RIC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resin_identification_code "Resin identification code") and are identified as "Other", 7 on the RIC list. Products made from polycarbonate can contain the precursor monomer [bisphenol A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisphenol_A "Bisphenol A") (BPA).
## Structure
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Structure")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/DINWOM10.png/250px-DINWOM10.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:DINWOM10.png)Structure of dicarbonate (PhOC(O)OC6H4 )2CMe2 derived from bis(phenol-A) and two equivalents of phenol.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-Perez-5) This molecule reflects a subunit of a typical polycarbonate derived from bis(phenol-A).
Carbonate esters have planar OC(OC)2 cores, which confer rigidity. The unique O=C bond is short (1.173 Å in the depicted example), while the C-O bonds are more ether-like (the bond distances of 1.326 Å for the example depicted). Polycarbonates received their name because they are [polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymers "Polymers") containing [carbonate groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonate_ester "Carbonate ester") (−O−(C=O)−O−). A balance of useful features, including temperature resistance, impact resistance and optical properties, positions polycarbonates between [commodity plastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_plastics "Commodity plastics") and [engineering plastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_plastic "Engineering plastic").
## Production
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Production")]
### Phosgene route
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Phosgene route")]
The main polycarbonate material is produced by the reaction of [bisphenol A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisphenol_A "Bisphenol A") (BPA) and [phosgene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosgene "Phosgene") (COCl  
2). The overall reaction can be written as follows:     [![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Polycarbonatsynthese.svg/500px-Polycarbonatsynthese.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Polycarbonatsynthese.svg)
The first step of the synthesis involves treatment of bisphenol A with [sodium hydroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_hydroxide "Sodium hydroxide"), which [deprotonates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deprotonates "Deprotonates") the [hydroxyl groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxyl_group "Hydroxyl group") of the bisphenol A.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-Ullmann-6)     (HOC6H4)2CMe2 + 2 NaOH → Na2(OC6H4)2CMe2 + 2 H2O
The [diphenoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenoxide "Phenoxide") (Na2(OC6H4)2CMe2) reacts with phosgene to give a [chloroformate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroformate "Chloroformate"), which subsequently is attacked by another phenoxide. The net reaction from the diphenoxide is:     Na2(OC6H4)2CMe2 + COCl2 → 1⁄ _n_ [OC(OC6H4)2CMe2]_n_ + 2 NaCl
In this way, approximately one billion kilograms of polycarbonate is produced annually. Many other [diols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diol "Diol") have been tested in place of bisphenol A, e.g. 1,1-bis(4-hydroxyphenyl)cyclohexane and [dihydroxybenzophenone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihydroxybenzophenone "Dihydroxybenzophenone"). The cyclohexane is used as a comonomer to suppress crystallisation tendency of the BPA-derived product. [Tetrabromobisphenol A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrabromobisphenol_A "Tetrabromobisphenol A") is used to enhance fire resistance. [Tetramethylcyclobutanediol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetramethylcyclobutanediol "Tetramethylcyclobutanediol") has been developed as a replacement for BPA.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-Ullmann-6)
### Transesterification route
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Transesterification route")]
An alternative route to polycarbonates entails [transesterification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transesterification "Transesterification") from BPA and [diphenyl carbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diphenyl_carbonate "Diphenyl carbonate"):     (HOC6H4)2CMe2 + (C6H5O)2CO → 1⁄ _n_ [OC(OC6H4)2CMe2]_n_ + 2 C6H5OH[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-Ullmann-6)
## Properties and processing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Properties and processing")]
Polycarbonate is a durable material. Although it has high impact-resistance, it has low scratch-resistance. Therefore, a hard coating is applied to polycarbonate [eyewear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyewear "Eyewear") [lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrective_lens "Corrective lens") and polycarbonate exterior automotive components. The characteristics of polycarbonate compare to those of [polymethyl methacrylate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymethyl_methacrylate "Polymethyl methacrylate") (PMMA, acrylic), but polycarbonate is [tougher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toughness "Toughness") and will hold up longer to extreme temperature. Thermally processed material is usually totally amorphous,[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-7) and as a result is highly [transparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_\(optics\) "Transparency \(optics\)") to [visible light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_light "Visible light"), with better light transmission than many kinds of glass.
Polycarbonate has a [glass transition temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_transition_temperature "Glass transition temperature") of about 147°C (297°F),[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-8) so it softens gradually above this point and flows above about 155°C (311°F).[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-city-9) Tools must be held at high temperatures, generally above 80°C (176°F) to make strain-free and stress-free products. Low [molecular mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_mass "Molecular mass") grades are easier to mold than higher grades, but their strength is lower as a result. The toughest grades have the highest molecular mass, but are more difficult to process.
Unlike most thermoplastics, polycarbonate can undergo large plastic deformations without cracking or breaking. As a result, it can be processed and formed at room temperature using [sheet metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheet_metal "Sheet metal") techniques, such as bending on a [brake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brake_\(sheet_metal_bending\) "Brake \(sheet metal bending\)"). Even for sharp angle bends with a tight radius, heating may not be necessary. This makes it valuable in prototyping applications where transparent or electrically non-conductive parts are needed, which cannot be made from sheet metal. [PMMA/Acrylic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poly\(methyl_methacrylate\) "Poly\(methyl methacrylate\)"), which is similar in appearance to polycarbonate, is brittle and cannot be bent at room temperature.
Main transformation techniques for polycarbonate resin(s):
  * [extrusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrusion "Extrusion") into tubes, rods and other profiles including multiwall
  * extrusion with cylinders ([calenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calender "Calender")) into sheets (0.5–20mm (0.020–0.787in)) and films (below 1mm (0.039in)), which can be used directly or manufactured into other shapes using [thermoforming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoforming "Thermoforming") or secondary [fabrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing "Manufacturing") techniques, such as bending, drilling, or routing. Due to its chemical properties it is not conducive to laser-cutting.
  * [injection molding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injection_molding "Injection molding") into ready articles


Polycarbonate may become [brittle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittleness "Brittleness") when exposed to ionizing radiation above 25 [kGy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_\(unit\) "Gray \(unit\)") (kJ/kg).[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-10)

## Applications
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Applications")]
### Electronic components
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Electronic components")]
Polycarbonate is mainly used for electronic applications that capitalize on its collective safety features. A good electrical insulator with heat-resistant and flame-retardant properties, it is used in products associated with power systems and telecommunications hardware. It can serve as a [dielectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric "Dielectric") in high-stability [capacitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor "Capacitor").[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-Ullmann-6) Commercial manufacture of polycarbonate capacitors mostly stopped after the sole manufacturer of capacitor-grade polycarbonate film, [Bayer AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_AG "Bayer AG"), stopped producing it at the end of 2000.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-11)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-12)
### Construction materials
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Construction materials")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Polycarbonate_Greenhouse-00.jpg/250px-Polycarbonate_Greenhouse-00.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Polycarbonate_Greenhouse-00.jpg)Polycarbonate sheeting in a greenhouse
The second largest consumer of polycarbonates is the construction industry, e.g. for domelights, flat or curved glazing, roofing sheets and [sound walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_wall "Sound wall"). Polycarbonates are used to create materials used in buildings that must be durable but light.
### 3D printing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: 3D printing")]
Polycarbonates are used extensively in 3D FDM printing, producing durable strong plastic products with a high melting point. Polycarbonate is relatively difficult for casual hobbyists to print compared to thermoplastics such as [polylactic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polylactic_acid "Polylactic acid") (PLA) or [acrylonitrile butadiene styrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrylonitrile_butadiene_styrene "Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene") (ABS) because of the high melting point, difficulty with print bed adhesion, tendency to warp during printing, and tendency to absorb moisture in humid environments. Despite these issues, 3D printing using polycarbonates is common in the professional community.
### Data storage
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Data storage")]
A major polycarbonate market is the production of [compact discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_disc "Compact disc"), [DVDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD "DVD"), and [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray "Blu-ray") discs.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-13) These discs are produced by injection-molding polycarbonate into a mold cavity that has on one side a metal stamper containing a negative image of the disc data, while the other mold side is a mirrored surface. Typical products of sheet/film production include applications in advertisement (signs, displays, poster protection).[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-Ullmann-6)
### Automotive, aircraft, and security components
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Automotive, aircraft, and security components")]
In the automotive industry, injection-molded polycarbonate can produce very smooth surfaces that make it well-suited for [sputter deposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputter_deposition "Sputter deposition") or [evaporation deposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporation_\(deposition\) "Evaporation \(deposition\)") of aluminium without the need for a base-coat. Decorative bezels and optical reflectors are commonly made of polycarbonate. Its low weight and high impact resistance have made polycarbonate the dominant material for automotive headlamp lenses. However, automotive headlamps require outer surface coatings because of its low scratch resistance and susceptibility to ultraviolet degradation (yellowing). The use of polycarbonate in automotive applications is limited to low stress applications. Stress from fasteners, [plastic welding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_welding "Plastic welding") and molding render polycarbonate susceptible to [stress corrosion cracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_corrosion_cracking "Stress corrosion cracking") when it comes in contact with certain accelerants such as salt water and [plastisol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastisol "Plastisol"). It can be laminated to make [bullet-proof "glass"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet-proof_glass "Bullet-proof glass"), although "bullet-resistant" is more accurate for the thinner windows, such as are used in bullet-resistant windows in automobiles. The thicker barriers of transparent plastic used in teller's windows and barriers in banks are also polycarbonate.
So-called "theft-proof" large plastic packaging for smaller items, which cannot be opened by hand, is typically made from polycarbonate.
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/F-22_Raptor_and_pilot_at_Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Miramar_25_Jun_2010.jpg/250px-F-22_Raptor_and_pilot_at_Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Miramar_25_Jun_2010.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:F-22_Raptor_and_pilot_at_Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Miramar_25_Jun_2010.jpg)Lockheed Martin F-22 cockpit canopy
The cockpit canopy of the [Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor "Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor") jet fighter is fabricated from high optical quality polycarbonate. It is the largest item of its type.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-15)
### Niche applications
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Niche applications")]
Polycarbonate, being a versatile material with attractive processing and physical properties, has attracted myriad smaller applications. The use of injection molded drinking bottles, glasses and food containers is common, but the use of BPA in the manufacture of polycarbonate has stirred concerns (see [Potential hazards in food contact applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#Potential_hazards_in_food_contact_applications)), leading to development and use of "BPA-free" plastics in various formulations.
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Laboratory_protection_goggles-blue.jpg/250px-Laboratory_protection_goggles-blue.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Laboratory_protection_goggles-blue.jpg)Laboratory safety goggles
Polycarbonate is commonly used in eye protection, as well as in other projectile-resistant viewing and lighting applications that would normally indicate the use of [glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass "Glass"), but require much higher impact-resistance. Polycarbonate lenses also protect the eye from [UV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyeglass#Polycarbonate "Eyeglass") light. Many kinds of lenses are manufactured from polycarbonate, including automotive headlamp lenses, lighting lenses, [sunglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunglass "Sunglass")/[eyeglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyeglass "Eyeglass") [lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrective_lens "Corrective lens"), [camera lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camera_lenses "Camera lenses"), swimming goggles and SCUBA masks, and safety glasses/goggles/visors including visors in sporting helmets/masks and police [riot gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_gear "Riot gear") (helmet visors, riot shields, etc.). Windscreens in small motorized vehicles are commonly made of polycarbonate, such as for motorcycles, ATVs, golf carts, and small airplanes and helicopters.
The light weight of polycarbonate as opposed to glass has led to development of electronic display screens that replace glass with polycarbonate, for use in mobile and portable devices. Such displays include newer [e-ink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-ink "E-ink") and some LCD screens, though CRT, plasma screen and other LCD technologies generally still require glass for its higher melting temperature and its ability to be etched in finer detail.
As more and more governments are restricting the use of glass in pubs and clubs due to the increased incidence of [glassings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glassing "Glassing"), polycarbonate glasses are becoming popular for serving alcohol because of their strength, durability, and glass-like feel.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-16)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-17)
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Lamy_2000_fountain_pen%2C_EF_semi_hooded_nib.jpg/250px-Lamy_2000_fountain_pen%2C_EF_semi_hooded_nib.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Lamy_2000_fountain_pen,_EF_semi_hooded_nib.jpg)[Lamy 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamy#2000 "Lamy") piston fill pen made of polycarbonate and stainless steel, launched in 1966 and still in production
Other miscellaneous items include durable, lightweight luggage, [MP3/digital audio player cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_audio_player "Digital audio player"), [ocarinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocarina "Ocarina"), computer cases, [riot shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_shields "Riot shields"), instrument panels, tealight candle containers and food blender jars. Many toys and hobby items are made from polycarbonate parts, like fins, gyro mounts, and flybar locks in [radio-controlled helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio-controlled_helicopter "Radio-controlled helicopter"),[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-18) and transparent [LEGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LEGO "LEGO") ([ABS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrylonitrile_butadiene_styrene#Applications "Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene") is used for opaque pieces).[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-19)
Standard polycarbonate resins are not suitable for long term exposure to UV radiation. To overcome this, the primary resin can have UV stabilisers added. These grades are sold as UV stabilized polycarbonate to injection moulding and extrusion companies. Other applications, including polycarbonate sheets, may have the anti-UV layer added as a special coating or a [coextrusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastics_extrusion#Coextrusion "Plastics extrusion") for enhanced [weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer_degradation "Polymer degradation") resistance.
Polycarbonate is also used as a printing substrate for [nameplate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nameplate "Nameplate") and other forms of industrial grade under printed products. The polycarbonate provides a barrier to wear, the elements, and fading.
#### Medical applications
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Medical applications")]
Many polycarbonate grades are used in medical applications and comply with both ISO 10993-1 and USP Class VI standards (occasionally referred to as PC-ISO). Class VI is the most stringent of the six USP ratings. These grades can be sterilized using steam at 120°C, [gamma radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_radiation "Gamma radiation"), or by the [ethylene oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene_oxide "Ethylene oxide") (EtO) method.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-20) Trinseo strictly limits all its plastics with regard to medical applications.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-22) Aliphatic polycarbonates have been developed with improved biocompatibility and degradability for nanomedicine applications.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-23)
### Mobile phones
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Mobile phones")]
Some smartphone manufacturers use polycarbonate. Nokia used polycarbonate in their phones starting with the [N9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia_N9 "Nokia N9")'s unibody case in 2011.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-24) This practice continued with various phones in the [Lumia series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumia_series "Lumia series"). Samsung started using polycarbonate with [Galaxy S III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Galaxy_S_III "Samsung Galaxy S III")'s _hyperglaze_ -branded removable battery cover in 2012. This practice continues with various phones in the [Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Galaxy "Samsung Galaxy") series. Apple started using polycarbonate with the [iPhone 5C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_5C "IPhone 5C")'s [unibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unibody "Unibody") case in 2013.
Benefits over glass and metal back covers include durability against shattering (advantage over glass), bending and scratching (advantage over metal), shock absorption, low manufacturing costs, and no interference with radio signals and [wireless charging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_charging "Wireless charging") (advantage over metal).[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-aa-materials-25) Polycarbonate back covers are available in glossy or matte [surface textures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_texture "Surface texture").[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-aa-materials-25)
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: History")]
Polycarbonates were first discovered in 1898 by [Alfred Einhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Einhorn "Alfred Einhorn"), a German scientist working at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich "LMU Munich").[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-26) However, after 30 years' laboratory research, this class of materials was abandoned without commercialization. Research resumed in 1953, when [Hermann Schnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Josef_Schnell "Hermann Josef Schnell") at [Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer "Bayer") in Uerdingen, Germany patented the first linear polycarbonate. The brand name "Makrolon" was registered in 1955.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-KotlerPfoertsch2010-27)
Also in 1953, and one week after the invention at Bayer, [Daniel Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Fox_\(chemist\) "Daniel Fox \(chemist\)") at [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric "General Electric") (GE) in Pittsfield, Massachusetts, independently synthesized a [branched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branching_\(polymer_chemistry\) "Branching \(polymer chemistry\)") polycarbonate. Both companies filed for U.S. patents in 1955, and agreed that the company lacking priority would be granted a license to the technology.[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-28)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-29)
Patent priority was resolved in Bayer's favor, and Bayer began commercial production under the trade name Makrolon in 1958. GE began production under the name Lexan in 1960, creating the [GE Plastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Plastics "GE Plastics") division in 1973.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-30)
After 1970, the original brownish polycarbonate tint was improved to "glass-clear".

## Potential hazards in food contact applications
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Potential hazards in food contact applications")]
Main articles: [Bisphenol A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisphenol_A "Bisphenol A") and [Endocrine disruptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_disruptor "Endocrine disruptor")
The use of polycarbonate containers for the purpose of food storage is controversial. The basis of this controversy is their hydrolysis (degradation by water, often referred to as leaching) occurring at high temperature, releases [bisphenol A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisphenol_A "Bisphenol A"):      1⁄ _n_ [OC(OC6H4)2CMe2]_n_ + H2O → (HOC6H4)2CMe2 + CO2
More than 100 studies have explored the bioactivity of bisphenol A derived from polycarbonates. Bisphenol A appeared to be released from polycarbonate animal cages into water at room temperature and it may have been responsible for enlargement of the reproductive organs of female mice.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-31) However, the animal cages used in the research were fabricated from industrial grade polycarbonate, rather than FDA food grade polycarbonate.
An analysis of the literature on bisphenol A leachate low-dose effects by vom Saal and Hughes published in August 2005 seems to have found a suggestive correlation between the source of funding and the conclusion drawn. Industry-funded studies tend to find no significant effects whereas government-funded studies tend to find significant effects.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-32)
[Sodium hypochlorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_hypochlorite "Sodium hypochlorite") bleach and other alkali cleaners catalyze the release of the bisphenol A from polycarbonate containers.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-33)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-34) Polycarbonate is incompatible with ammonia and acetone. [Alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(chemistry\) "Alcohol \(chemistry\)") is a recommended [organic solvent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_solvent "Organic solvent") for cleaning grease and oils from polycarbonate.
## Environmental impact
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Environmental impact")]
### Disposal
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=18 "Edit section: Disposal")]
Studies have shown that at temperatures above 70°C, and high humidity, polycarbonate will hydrolyze to [bisphenol A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisphenol_A "Bisphenol A") (BPA). After about 30 days at 85°C/96% RH, surface crystals are formed which for 70% consisted of BPA.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-35) BPA is a compound that is currently on the list of potential environmental hazardous chemicals. It is on the watch list of many countries, such as United States and Germany.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-:12-36)      —(—OC6H4)2C(CH3)2CO—)— _n_ + H2O ⟶ (CH3)2C(C6H4OH)2 + CO2
The leaching of BPA from polycarbonate can also occur at environmental temperature and normal pH (in landfills).The amount of leaching increases as the polycarbonate parts get older. A study found that the decomposition of BPA in landfills (under anaerobic conditions) will not occur.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-:12-36) It will therefore be persistent in landfills. Eventually, it will find its way into water bodies and contribute to aquatic pollution.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-:12-36)[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-37)
### Photo-oxidation of polycarbonate
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=19 "Edit section: Photo-oxidation of polycarbonate")]
In the presence of UV light, oxidation of this polymer yields compounds such as [ketones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketones "Ketones"), [phenols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenols "Phenols"), o-[phenoxybenzoic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenoxybenzoic_acid?action=edit&redlink=1 "Phenoxybenzoic acid \(page does not exist\)"), [benzyl alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzyl_alcohol "Benzyl alcohol") and other unsaturated compounds. This has been suggested through kinetic and spectral studies. The yellow color formed after long exposure to sun can also be related to further oxidation of phenolic end group[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-:2-38)     (OC6H4)2C(CH3)2CO)_n_ + O2 R*⟶ (OC6H4)2C(CH3CH2)CO)_n_
This product can be further oxidized to form smaller unsaturated compounds. This can proceed via two different pathways, the products formed depends on which mechanism takes place.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-39) 

Pathway A
    (OC6H4)2C(CH3CH2)CO + O2 H*⟶ HO(OC6H4)OCO + CH3COCH2(OC6H4)OCO 

Pathway B
    (OC6H4)2C(CH3CH2)CO)n + O2 H*⟶ OCO(OC6H4)CH2OH + OCO(OC6H4)COCH3
### Photo-aging reaction
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=20 "Edit section: Photo-aging reaction")]
Photo-aging is another degradation route for polycarbonates. Polycarbonate molecules (such as the aromatic ring) absorb UV radiation. This absorbed energy causes cleavage of covalent bonds which initiates the photo-aging process. The reaction can be propagated via side chain oxidation, ring oxidation or [photo-Fries rearrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fries_rearrangement#Photo-Fries_rearrangement "Fries rearrangement"). Products formed include [phenyl salicylate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenyl_salicylate "Phenyl salicylate"), [dihydroxybenzophenone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihydroxybenzophenone "Dihydroxybenzophenone") groups, and [hydroxydiphenyl ether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxydiphenyl_ether?action=edit&redlink=1 "Hydroxydiphenyl ether \(page does not exist\)") groups.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-:2-38)[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-:3-40)[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-41)     (C16H14O3)_n_ ⟶ C16H17O3 + C13H10O3
### Thermal degradation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=21 "Edit section: Thermal degradation")]
Waste polycarbonate will degrade at high temperatures to form solid, liquid and gaseous pollutants. A study showed that the products were about 40–50 wt.% liquid, 14–16 wt.% gases, while 34–43 wt.% remained as solid residue. Liquid products contained mainly phenol derivatives (~75 wt.%) and bisphenol (~10 wt.%) also present.[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-:3-40) Polycarbonate, however, can be safely used as a carbon source in the steel-making industry.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-42)
Phenol derivatives are environmental pollutants, classified as [volatile organic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatile_organic_compounds "Volatile organic compounds") (VOC). Studies show they are likely to facilitate ground level ozone formation and increase photo-chemical smog.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-43) In aquatic bodies, they can potentially accumulate in organisms. They are persistent in landfills, do not readily evaporate and would remain in the atmosphere.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-44)
### Effect of fungi
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Polycarbonate&action=edit&section=22 "Edit section: Effect of fungi")]
In 2001 a species of fungus in [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize "Belize"), _[Geotrichum candidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geotrichum_candidum "Geotrichum candidum")_ , was found to consume the polycarbonate found in [compact discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_disc "Compact disc") (CD).[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarbonate#cite_note-45) This has prospects for [bioremediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioremediation "Bioremediation"). However, this effect has not been reproduced.
