Chemical compound
**Silicon nitride** is a [chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical "Chemical") compound of the elements [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon "Silicon") and [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen "Nitrogen"). Si  
3N  
4 (_trisilicon tetranitride_) is the most thermodynamically stable and commercially important of the silicon nitrides,[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-6) and the term "silicon nitride" commonly refers to this specific composition. It is a white, high-melting-point solid that is relatively chemically inert, being attacked by dilute [HF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_fluoride "Hydrogen fluoride") and hot [H  
3PO  
4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphoric_acid "Phosphoric acid"). It is very hard (8.5 on the [Mohs scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohs_scale_of_mineral_hardness#Intermediate_hardness "Mohs scale of mineral hardness")). It has a high thermal stability with strong optical nonlinearities for all-optical applications.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-7)

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Production")]
Silicon nitride is prepared by heating powdered silicon between 1300°C and 1400°C in a nitrogen atmosphere:     3 Si + 2 N  
2 → Si  
3N  
4
The silicon sample weight increases progressively due to the chemical combination of silicon and nitrogen. Without an iron [catalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis "Catalysis"), the reaction is complete after several hours (~7), when no further weight increase due to nitrogen absorption (per gram of silicon) is detected.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
In addition to Si  
3N  
4, several other silicon nitride phases (with chemical formulas corresponding to varying degrees of nitridation/Si oxidation state) have been reported in the literature. These include the gaseous [disilicon mononitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disilicon_mononitride?action=edit&redlink=1 "Disilicon mononitride \(page does not exist\)") (Si  
2N), [silicon mononitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_mononitride?action=edit&redlink=1 "Silicon mononitride \(page does not exist\)") (SiN) and [silicon sesquinitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_sesquinitride?action=edit&redlink=1 "Silicon sesquinitride \(page does not exist\)") (Si  
2N  
3), each of which are stoichiometric phases. As with other [refractories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refractory "Refractory"), the products obtained in these high-temperature syntheses depends on the reaction conditions (e.g. time, temperature, and starting materials including the reactants and container materials), as well as the mode of purification. However, the existence of the sesquinitride has since come into question.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-8)
It can also be prepared by the [diimide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diimide "Diimide") route:[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-hist-9)      SiCl  
4 + 6 NH  
3 → Si(NH)  
2 + 4 NH  
4Cl(s) at 0°C     3 Si(NH)  
2 → Si  
3N  
4 + N  
2 + 3 H  
2(g) at 1000°C
[Carbothermal reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbothermal_reduction "Carbothermal reduction") of silicon dioxide in a nitrogen atmosphere at 1400–1450°C has also been examined:[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-hist-9)     3 SiO  
2 + 6 C + 2 N  
2 → Si  
3N  
4 + 6 CO
The nitridation of silicon powder was developed in the 1950s, following the "rediscovery" of silicon nitride and was the first large-scale method for powder production. However, use of low-purity raw silicon caused contamination of silicon nitride by [silicates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicate "Silicate") and [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron "Iron"). The diimide decomposition results in amorphous silicon nitride, which needs further annealing under nitrogen at 1400–1500°C to convert it to a crystalline powder; this is now the second-most-important route for commercial production. The carbothermal reduction was the earliest used method for silicon nitride production and is now considered as the most-cost-effective industrial route to high-purity silicon nitride powder.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-hist-9)

For deposition of silicon nitride layers on semiconductor (usually silicon) substrates, two methods are used:[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-prop-10)

Plasma-enhanced [atomic layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_layer_deposition "Atomic layer deposition") chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) technology, which works at rather low temperature (≤ 250 °C) and vacuum conditions.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-:0-13) Examples include bis(diethylamino)silane as silicon precursor and plasma of N2 as reactant.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-:0-13)

## Processing
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Processing")]
Silicon nitride is difficult to produce as a bulk material—it cannot be heated over 1850°C, which is well below its [melting point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_point "Melting point"), due to [dissociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociation_\(chemistry\) "Dissociation \(chemistry\)") to silicon and nitrogen. Therefore, application of conventional [hot press sintering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_isostatic_pressing "Hot isostatic pressing") techniques is problematic. Bonding of silicon nitride powders can be achieved at lower temperatures through adding materials called sintering aids or "binders", which commonly induce a degree of liquid phase sintering.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-azom-16) A cleaner alternative is to use [spark plasma sintering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spark_plasma_sintering "Spark plasma sintering"), where heating is conducted very rapidly (seconds) by passing pulses of electric current through the compacted powder. Dense silicon nitride compacts have been obtained by this techniques at temperatures 1500–1700°C.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-17)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-18)

There exist three [crystallographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallographic "Crystallographic") structures of silicon nitride (Si  
3N  
4), designated as α, β and γ phases.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-19) The α and β [phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_\(matter\) "Phase \(matter\)") are the most common forms of Si  
3N  
4, and can be produced under normal pressure condition. The γ phase can only be synthesized under high pressures and temperatures and has a hardness of 35GPa.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-20)[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-21)
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Si3N4strength.jpg/250px-Si3N4strength.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Si3N4strength.jpg)
The α- and β-Si  
3N  
4 have [trigonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal "Trigonal") ([Pearson symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_symbol "Pearson symbol") hP28, [space group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_group "Space group") P31c, No. 159) and [hexagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_crystal_system "Hexagonal crystal system") (hP14, P63, No. 173) structures, respectively, which are built up by corner-sharing SiN  
4 [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedron "Tetrahedron"). They can be regarded as consisting of layers of silicon and nitrogen atoms in the sequence ABAB... or ABCDABCD... in β-Si  
3N  
4 and α-Si  
3N  
4, respectively. The AB layer is the same in the α and β phases, and the CD layer in the α phase is related to AB by a c-glide plane. The Si  
3N  
4 tetrahedra in β-Si  
3N  
4 are interconnected in such a way that tunnels are formed, running parallel with the c axis of the unit cell. Due to the c-glide plane that relates AB to CD, the α structure contains cavities instead of tunnels. The cubic γ-Si  
3N  
4 is often designated as c modification in the literature, in analogy with the cubic modification of [boron nitride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boron_nitride "Boron nitride") (c-BN). It has a [spinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinel "Spinel")-type structure in which two silicon atoms each coordinate six nitrogen atoms octahedrally, and one silicon atom coordinates four nitrogen atoms tetrahedrally.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-22)
The longer stacking sequence results in the α-phase having higher hardness than the β-phase. However, the α-phase is chemically unstable compared with the β-phase. At high temperatures when a liquid phase is present, the α-phase always transforms into the β-phase. Therefore, β-Si  
3N  
4 is the major form used in Si  
3N  
4 ceramics.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-23) [Abnormal grain growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abnormal_grain_growth "Abnormal grain growth") may occur in doped β-Si  
3N  
4, whereby abnormally large elongated grains form in a matrix of finer equiaxed grains and can serve as a technique to enhance fracture toughness in this material by crack bridging.[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-24) [Abnormal grain growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abnormal_grain_growth "Abnormal grain growth") in doped silicon nitride arises due to additive-enhanced diffusion and results in composite microstructures, which can also be considered as "in-situ composites" or "self-reinforced materials.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-25)
In addition to the crystalline polymorphs of silicon nitride, glassy amorphous materials may be formed as the pyrolysis products of [preceramic polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preceramic_polymers "Preceramic polymers"), most often containing varying amounts of residual carbon (hence they are more appropriately considered as silicon carbonitrides). Specifically, polycarbosilazane can be readily converted to an amorphous form of silicon carbonitride based material upon pyrolysis, with valuable implications in the processing of silicon nitride materials through processing techniques more commonly used for polymers.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-26)
## Applications
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Applications")]
In general, the main issue with applications of silicon nitride has not been technical performance, but cost. As the cost has come down, the number of production applications is accelerating.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-ornl-27)
### Automotive industry
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Automotive industry")]
One of the major applications of sintered silicon nitride is in engine parts. It can be used in [diesel engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine "Diesel engine"), [glowplugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glow_plug_\(diesel_engine\) "Glow plug \(diesel engine\)") for sped up start-up times; precombustion chambers (swirl chambers) to reduce emissions, start-up time and noise; and [turbochargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbochargers "Turbochargers") to reduce engine lag and emissions. In [spark-ignition engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spark-ignition_engine "Spark-ignition engine"), silicon nitride is used for [rocker arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocker_arm "Rocker arm") pads for lower [wear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wear "Wear"), turbocharger turbines for lower inertia and less engine lag, and in [exhaust gas control valves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine "Internal combustion engine") for increased acceleration. Currently, it is estimated that more than 300,000 sintered silicon nitride turbochargers are made annually.
Silicon nitride is used in some high-performance automotive ceramic coatings for protecting paint.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-hist-9)[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-azom-16)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-ornl-27)
### Bearings
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Bearings")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Si3N4bearings.jpg/250px-Si3N4bearings.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Si3N4bearings.jpg)Si3N4 bearing parts
Silicon nitride bearings are both full ceramic bearings and [ceramic hybrid bearings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_bearing#Ceramic_hybrid_ball_bearings_using_ceramic_balls "Ball bearing") with balls in ceramics and [races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(bearing\) "Race \(bearing\)") in steel. Silicon nitride [ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic "Ceramic") have good [shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_\(mechanics\) "Shock \(mechanics\)") resistance compared to other ceramics. Therefore, ball bearings made of silicon nitride ceramic are used in performance [bearings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bearing_\(mechanical\) "Bearing \(mechanical\)"). A representative example is use of silicon nitride bearings in the main engines of the NASA's [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle "Space Shuttle").[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-28)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-29)
Since silicon nitride ball bearings are harder than metal, this reduces contact with the bearing track. This results in 80% less friction, three to ten times longer lifetime, 80% higher speed, 60% less weight, the ability to operate with lubrication starvation, higher corrosion resistance and higher operation temperature, as compared to traditional metal bearings.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-ornl-27) Silicon nitride balls weigh 79% less than [tungsten carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten_carbide "Tungsten carbide") balls. Silicon nitride ball bearings can be found in high end automotive bearings, industrial bearings, [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine "Wind turbine"), motorsports, bicycles, rollerblades and [skateboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboard "Skateboard"). Silicon nitride bearings are especially useful in applications where corrosion or electric or magnetic fields prohibit the use of metals, for example, in tidal flow meters, where seawater attack is a problem, or in electric field seekers.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-azom-16)
Si3N4 was first demonstrated as a superior bearing in 1972 but did not reach production until nearly 1990 because of challenges associated with reducing the cost. Since 1990, the cost has been reduced substantially as production volume has increased. Although Si  
3N  
4 bearings are still two to five times more expensive than the best steel bearings, their superior performance and life are justifying rapid adoption. Around 15–20million Si  
3N  
4 bearing balls were produced in the U.S. in 1996 for machine tools and many other applications. Growth is estimated at 40% per year, but could be even higher if ceramic bearings are selected for consumer applications such as in-line skates and computer disk drives.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-ornl-27)
NASA testing says ceramic-hybrid bearings exhibit much lower fatigue (wear) life than standard all-steel bearings.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-30)
### High-temperature material
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: High-temperature material")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/Si3N4thruster.jpg/500px-Si3N4thruster.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Si3N4thruster.jpg)Silicon nitride thruster. Left: Mounted in test stand. Right: Being tested with H2/O2 propellants
Silicon nitride has long been used in high-temperature applications. In particular, it was identified as one of the few monolithic ceramic materials capable of surviving the severe thermal shock and thermal gradients generated in hydrogen/oxygen rocket engines. To demonstrate this capability in a complex configuration, NASA scientists used advanced rapid prototyping technology to fabricate a one-inch-diameter, single-piece combustion chamber/nozzle (thruster) component. The thruster was hot-fire tested with hydrogen/oxygen propellant and survived five cycles including a 5-minute cycle to a 1320°C material temperature.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-31)
In 2010 silicon nitride was used as the main material in the thrusters of the [JAXA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JAXA "JAXA") space probe [Akatsuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akatsuki_\(spacecraft\) "Akatsuki \(spacecraft\)").[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-32)
Silicon nitride was used for the "microshutters" developed for the [Near Infrared Spectrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-infrared_spectroscopy#Astronomical_spectroscopy "Near-infrared spectroscopy") aboard the [James Webb Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope "James Webb Space Telescope"). According to NASA: The "operating temperature is cryogenic so the device has to be able to operate at extremely cold temperatures. Another challenge was developing shutters that would be able to: open and close repeatedly without fatigue; open individually; and open wide enough to meet the science requirements of the instrument. Silicon nitride was chosen for use in the microshutters, because of its high strength and resistance to fatigue." This microshutter system allows the instrument to observe and analyze up to 100 celestial objects simultaneously.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-33)
### Medical
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Medical")]
Silicon nitride has many orthopedic applications.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-one-34)[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-three-35) The material is also an alternative to [PEEK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEEK "PEEK") (polyether ether ketone) and [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium "Titanium"), which are used for [spinal fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_fusion "Spinal fusion") devices (with latter being relatively expensive).[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-four-36)[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-six-37) It is silicon nitride's [hydrophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophilic "Hydrophilic"), [microtextured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribology "Tribology") surface that contributes to the material's strength, durability and reliability compared to PEEK and titanium.[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-three-35)[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-four-36)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-seven-38) Certain compositions of this material exhibit anti-bacterial,[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-39) anti-fungal,[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-40) or anti-viral properties.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-41)
### Metal working and cutting
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Metal working and cutting")]
The first major application of Si  
3N  
4 was abrasive and [cutting tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_tool_material "Cutting tool material"). Bulk, monolithic silicon nitride is used as a material for [cutting tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_tool "Cutting tool"), due to its hardness, thermal stability, and resistance to [wear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wear "Wear"). It is especially recommended for high speed [machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machining "Machining") of [cast iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cast_iron "Cast iron"). Hot hardness, fracture toughness and thermal shock resistance mean that sintered silicon nitride can cut cast iron, hard steel and nickel based alloys with surface speeds up to 25 times quicker than those obtained with conventional materials such as tungsten carbide.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-azom-16) The use of Si  
3N  
4 cutting tools has had a dramatic effect on manufacturing output. For example, face milling of gray cast iron with silicon nitride inserts doubled the cutting speed, increased tool life from one part to six parts per edge, and reduced the average cost of inserts by 50%, as compared to traditional [tungsten carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten_carbide "Tungsten carbide") tools.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-hist-9)[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-ornl-27)
### Electronics
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Electronics")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Locos_%28microtechnology%29_process.svg/250px-Locos_%28microtechnology%29_process.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Locos_\(microtechnology\)_process.svg)Example of [local silicon oxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOCOS "LOCOS") through an Si3N4 mask
Silicon nitride is often used as an [insulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_insulation "Electrical insulation") and chemical barrier in manufacturing [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuits "Integrated circuits"), to electrically isolate different structures or as an [etch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etching_\(microfabrication\) "Etching \(microfabrication\)") mask in [bulk micromachining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulk_micromachining "Bulk micromachining"). As a passivation layer for microchips, it is superior to [silicon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_dioxide "Silicon dioxide"), as it is a significantly better [diffusion barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_barrier "Diffusion barrier") against water molecules and [sodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium "Sodium") ions, two major sources of corrosion and instability in microelectronics. It is also used as a [dielectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric "Dielectric") between [polysilicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysilicon "Polysilicon") layers in [capacitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor "Capacitor") in analog chips.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-42)
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/AFM_%28used%29_cantilever_in_Scanning_Electron_Microscope%2C_magnification_1000x.GIF/250px-AFM_%28used%29_cantilever_in_Scanning_Electron_Microscope%2C_magnification_1000x.GIF?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:AFM_\(used\)_cantilever_in_Scanning_Electron_Microscope,_magnification_1000x.GIF)Si3N4 cantilever used in atomic force microscopes
Silicon nitride deposited by [LPCVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_vapor_deposition "Chemical vapor deposition") contains up to 8% hydrogen. It also experiences strong tensile [stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_\(physics\) "Stress \(physics\)"), which may crack films thicker than 200nm. However, it has higher [resistivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistivity "Resistivity") and dielectric strength than most insulators commonly available in microfabrication (1016 [Ω](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm "Ohm")·cm and 10MV/cm, respectively).[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-prop-10)
Not only silicon nitride, but also various ternary compounds of silicon, nitrogen and hydrogen (SiNxHy) are used as insulating layers. They are plasma deposited using the following reactions:[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-prop-10)     2 SiH  
4(g) + N  
2(g) → 2 SiNH(s) + 3 H  
2(g)      SiH  
4(g) + NH

2(g)
These SiNH films have much less tensile stress, but worse electrical properties (resistivity 106 to 1015Ω·cm, and dielectric strength 1 to 5MV/cm),[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-prop-10)[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-43) and are thermally stable to high temperatures under specific physical conditions. Silicon nitride is also used in the [xerographic process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerography "Xerography") as one of the layers of the photo drum.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-44) Silicon nitride is also used as an ignition source for domestic gas appliances.[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-45) Because of its good elastic properties, silicon nitride, along with silicon and silicon oxide, is the most popular material for [cantilevers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantilever "Cantilever") — the sensing elements of [atomic force microscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_force_microscope "Atomic force microscope").[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-46)
## Aspirational applications
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Aspirational applications")]
### Solar cells
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Solar cells")]
[Solar cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cell "Solar cell") are often coated with an [anti-reflective coating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-reflective_coating "Anti-reflective coating"). Silicon nitride can be used for this, and it is possible to adjust its [index of refraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_of_refraction "Index of refraction") by varying the parameters of the deposition process.[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-47)
### Photonic integrated circuits
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Photonic integrated circuits")]
[Photonic integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photonic_integrated_circuit "Photonic integrated circuit") can be produced with various materials, also called material platforms. Silicon nitride is one of those material platforms, next to, for example, [silicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_photonics "Silicon photonics") and [indium phosphide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indium_phosphide "Indium phosphide"). Silicon nitride photonic integrated circuits have a broad spectral coverage and features low light losses.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-48) This makes them highly suited to detectors, spectrometers, biosensors, and quantum computers. The lowest propagation losses reported in SiN (0.1dB/cm down to 0.1dB/m) have been achieved by LioniX International's TriPleX waveguides.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-49)
### High-stress membranes
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: High-stress membranes")]
Silicon nitride has emerged as a favorable platform for high-stress thin-film [membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane "Membrane") devices.[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-50)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-51) These devices have been used as sensing devices in a wide variety of a scientific experiments, including [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy "Spectroscopy") applications[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-52) and [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter "Dark matter") searches.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-53)
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Silicon_nitride&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: History")]
The first synthesis of silicon nitride was reported in 1857 by [Henri Etienne Sainte-Claire Deville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Etienne_Sainte-Claire_Deville "Henri Etienne Sainte-Claire Deville") and [Friedrich Wöhler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_W%C3%B6hler "Friedrich Wöhler").[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-54) In their method, silicon was heated in a crucible placed inside another crucible packed with carbon to reduce permeation of oxygen to the inner crucible. They reported a product they termed silicon nitride but without specifying its chemical composition. [Paul Schuetzenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Schuetzenberger "Paul Schuetzenberger") first reported a product with the composition of the tetranitride, Si  
3N  
4, in 1879 that was obtained by heating silicon with brasque (a paste made by mixing charcoal, coal, or coke with clay which is then used to line crucibles) in a blast furnace. In 1910, Ludwig Weiss and Theodor Engelhardt heated silicon under pure nitrogen to produce Si  
3N  
4.[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-55) E. Friederich and L. Sittig made Si3N4 in 1925 via carbothermal reduction under nitrogen, that is, by heating silica, carbon, and nitrogen at 1250–1300°C.
Silicon nitride remained merely a chemical curiosity for decades before it was used in commercial applications. From 1948 to 1952, the Carborundum Company, Niagara Falls, New York, applied for several [patents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent "Patent") on the manufacture and application of silicon nitride.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-hist-9) By 1958 [Haynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haynes_International "Haynes International") ([Union Carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Carbide "Union Carbide")) silicon nitride was in commercial production for [thermocouple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermocouple "Thermocouple") tubes, rocket nozzles, and boats and [crucibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucible "Crucible") for melting metals. British work on silicon nitride, started in 1953, was aimed at high-temperature parts of [gas turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine "Gas turbine") and resulted in the development of reaction-bonded silicon nitride and hot-pressed silicon nitride. In 1971, the [Advanced Research Project Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA "DARPA") of the [US Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense") placed a US$17million contract with [Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company "Ford Motor Company") and [Westinghouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Electric_\(1886\) "Westinghouse Electric \(1886\)") for two ceramic gas turbines.[[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-56)
Even though the properties of silicon nitride were well known, its natural occurrence was discovered only in the 1990s, as tiny inclusions (about 2[μm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%9Cm "Μm") × 0.5μm in size) in [meteorites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorite "Meteorite"). The mineral was named [nierite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nierite "Nierite") after a pioneer of [mass spectrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry "Mass spectrometry"), [Alfred O. C. Nier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_O._C._Nier "Alfred O. C. Nier").[[57]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-57) This mineral may have been detected earlier, again exclusively in meteorites, by Soviet geologists.[[58]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_nitride#cite_note-58)
