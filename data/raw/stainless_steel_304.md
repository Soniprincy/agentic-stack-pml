**SAE 304 stainless steel** is the most common [stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainless_steel "Stainless steel"). It is an alloy of [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron "Iron"), [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon "Carbon"), [chromium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium "Chromium") and [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel "Nickel"). It is an [austenitic stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austenitic_stainless_steel "Austenitic stainless steel"), and is therefore not magnetic. It is less electrically and thermally [conductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductive "Conductive") than [carbon steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_steel "Carbon steel"). It has higher [corrosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrosion "Corrosion") resistance than regular steel and is widely used because it is easy to form into various shapes.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-data_sheet-2)
The composition was developed by [W. H. Hatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Herbert_Hatfield "William Herbert Hatfield") at [Firth Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firth_Brown_Steels#Stainless_steels "Firth Brown Steels") in 1924 and was marketed under the trade name "Staybrite 18/8".[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-3)
It is specified by [SAE International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_International "SAE International") as part of its [SAE steel grades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_steel_grades "SAE steel grades"). It is also known as:[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-worldstain-4)
  * **4301-304-00-I** and **X5CrNi18-9** , the ISO 15510 name and designation.
  * **UNS S30400** in the [unified numbering system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_numbering_system "Unified numbering system").
  * **A2 stainless steel** outside the US, in accordance with [ISO 3506](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ISO_standards_3000%E2%80%934999 "List of ISO standards 3000–4999") for [fasteners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasteners "Fasteners").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-assda-Stainless_Steel_Fasteners-5)
  * **18/8** and **18/10** stainless steel (also written 18-8 and 18-10) in the commercial [tableware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tableware "Tableware") and fastener industries.
  * **SUS304** the Japanese JIS G4303 equivalent grade.
  * **1.4301** , the [EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Norm "European Norm") 10088 equivalent.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-6)
  * **06Cr19Ni10** and **ISC S30408** , the equivalent in Chinese GB/T 20878 and GB/T 17616 nomenclature.
  * **08Kh18N10** and **03Kh18N11** , the equivalents for 403 and 403L in [GOST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOST "GOST") nomenclature.

## Corrosion resistance
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=SAE_304_stainless_steel&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Corrosion resistance")]
304 stainless steel has excellent resistance to a wide range of atmospheric environments and many corrosive media. It is subject to pitting and crevice corrosion in warm [chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloride "Chloride") environments and to [stress corrosion cracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_corrosion_cracking "Stress corrosion cracking") above about 60°C (140°F). It is considered resistant to pitting corrosion in water with up to about 400mg/L chlorides at ambient temperatures, reducing to about 150mg/L at 60°C.
304 stainless steel is also very sensitive at room temperature to the [thiosulfate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiosulfate "Thiosulfate") anions released by the [oxidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidation "Oxidation") of [pyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrite "Pyrite") (as encountered in [acid mine drainage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_mine_drainage "Acid mine drainage")) and can undergo severe [pitting corrosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitting_corrosion "Pitting corrosion") problems when in close contact with [pyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrite "Pyrite")- or [sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfide "Sulfide")-rich [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay "Clay") materials exposed to oxidation.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
For more severe corrosion conditions, when 304 stainless steel is too sensitive to pitting or crevice corrosion by chlorides or general corrosion in acidic applications, it is commonly replaced by [316](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_316L_stainless_steel "SAE 316L stainless steel") stainless steel. 304 and 302 stainless steels are subject to chloride stress fracture failure when used in tropical salt water conditions such as oil or gas rigs. 316 stainless steel is the preferred alloy for these conditions.
## Mechanical properties
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=SAE_304_stainless_steel&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Mechanical properties")]
304 stainless steel cannot be heat treated, instead it can be strengthened by cold working. It is weakest in the annealed condition, and is strongest in the full-hard condition. The tensile yield strength ranges from 210 to 1,050MPa (30,000 to 153,000psi).
The density is 7,900kg/m3 (0.286lb/cuin), and its modulus of elasticity ranges from 183 to 200GPa (26.6×10~~^~~ 6 to 29.0×10~~^~~ 6psi).[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-8)
## Applications
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=SAE_304_stainless_steel&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Applications")]
[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Salad_bar.JPG/250px-Salad_bar.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Salad_bar.JPG)Staybrite containers in a salad bar
304 stainless steel is used for a variety of household and industrial applications such as food handling and processing equipment, screws,[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-assda-Stainless_Steel_Fasteners-5) machinery parts, utensils, and [exhaust manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhaust_manifold "Exhaust manifold"). 304 stainless steel is also used in the architectural field for exterior accents such as water and fire features. It is also a common coil material for vaporizers.
Early [SpaceX Starships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship "SpaceX Starship") used [SAE 301 stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_steel_grades#300_Series%E2%80%94austenitic_chromium-nickel_alloys "SAE steel grades") in their construction,[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-9) before moving over to SAE 304L for the [SN7 test tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship_development_history#SN7,_SN7.1,_and_SN7.2 "SpaceX Starship development history")[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-10) and [Starship SN8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_SN8 "Starship SN8") in 2020.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-11)
304 stainless steel was used to clad the [Gateway Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Arch "Gateway Arch") in St. Louis, Missouri.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-Offer-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-13)
## Carbon content
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=SAE_304_stainless_steel&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Carbon content")]
304, 304H, and 304L all possess the same nominal chromium and nickel content and also possess the same corrosion resistance, ease of fabrication, and weldability. The difference between 304, 304H, and 304L is the carbon content, which is <0.08, <0.1, and <0.035% respectively (also see UNS designations S30400, S30409, & S30403 respectively). 304 has both the H=High and the L=Low carbon variants.
The carbon content of 304H (UNS S30409) is restricted to 0.04–0.10%, which provides optimal high-temperature strength.
The carbon content of 304L (UNS 30403) is restricted to a maximum of 0.035%, which prevents sensitization during welding. Sensitization is the formation of chromium carbides along grain boundaries when stainless steel is exposed to temperatures in the approximate range of 480–820°C (900–1,500°F). The subsequent formation of chromium carbide results in reduced corrosion resistance along the grain boundary, leaving the stainless steel susceptible to unanticipated corrosion in an environment where 304 would be expected to be corrosion resistant. This grain boundary corrosive attack is known as intergranular corrosion.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-14)
The carbon content of 304 (UNS 30400) is restricted to a maximum of 0.08% and is not useful for corrosive applications where welding is required, such as tanks and pipes where corrosive solutions are involved, and 304L is preferred. Its lack of a minimum carbon content is not ideal for high-temperature applications where optimal strength is required, thus, 304H is usually preferred. Therefore 304 is typically restricted to bars that will be machined into components where welding is not required or thin sheets that are formed in articles such as kitchen sinks or cookware that are also not welded.
Carbon content has a strong influence on room temperature strength and thus the specified minimum tensile properties of 304L are 34MPa (5,000psi) lower than for 304. However, nitrogen also has a strong influence on room temperature strength and a tiny addition of nitrogen produces 304L with the same tensile strength as 304. Thus, practically all 304L is produced as dual certified 304/304L, meaning it meets the maximum carbon content of 304L and also meets the minimum tensile strength of 304.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAE_304_stainless_steel#cite_note-15)[_[full citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources#What_information_to_include "Wikipedia:Citing sources")_]
